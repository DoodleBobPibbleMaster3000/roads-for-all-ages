Who we are

A public-interest group for safer roads

OUR TYPE

Public interest

We fight for a collective good: safer roads. The benefit reaches every driver, cyclist and pedestrian, whether or not they ever join us.

HOW WE STARTED

A close call

One of our members was nearly hit head-on while biking home. When we looked at the data, we found the near miss was part of a national trend, and decided to act.

WHERE THINGS STAND

A patchwork

Every state sets its own renewal rules, so the checks an older driver faces depend on where they live. Illinois is among the strictest, with yearly road tests from age 87.

Why it matters

The problem is growing

From 2001 to 2021, the share of fatal crashes involving elderly drivers climbed from 11% to 19%. Better medicine and technology mean Americans live longer, and the senior population keeps growing. Too many drivers who are no longer fit to drive are still on the road, putting other motorists and pedestrians at risk.

Our mission is to prevent these accidents before they happen. We hold drivers accountable and make sure they are mentally and physically able to operate a motor vehicle safely. This protects seniors and younger drivers alike.

The plan

What the act does

PILLAR 1

Regular testing

Every driver 65 and older takes a recurring driving and skill test to keep their license.

PILLAR 2

National point system

One universal point system for senior drivers, the same in every state.

PILLAR 3

Real accountability

Clear, escalating consequences for drivers who skip their required tests.

The 6-point limit

Minor offense

3 pts

Failing to yield or stop, minor speeding

Major offense

6 pts

Reckless driving, DUI

Hit 6 points and your license is revoked for one year, with mandatory corrective driving tests.

Myths vs. Facts

The questions we hear most

We want this debate to run on evidence. Here are the most common objections to the Senior Driver Safety Act and how we answer them.

They say

"This is just discrimination against older people."

The facts

The act does not take anyone's license because of their age. It checks ability. Drivers who pass keep driving, exactly as before. NHTSA itself says age alone does not determine driving performance, which is why we test skills instead of setting a cutoff.

We already accept extra rules for one age group: graduated licensing for new teen drivers. Regular check-ins at the other end of a driving life follow the same idea.

Source: NHTSA, Countermeasures That Work

They say

"Senior drivers are some of the safest on the road."

The facts

Per licensed driver, older drivers do have fewer crashes, partly because many drive less. Per mile driven, the picture changes: fatal crash rates rise starting at ages 70 to 74 and are highest for drivers 85 and older.

And the overall share is climbing. Crashes involving a driver 65 or older went from 11% of all fatal crashes in 2001 to 19% in 2021.

Sources: IIHS Fatality Facts, NHTSA

They ask

"Why start at 65?"

The facts

Drivers 65 and older now make up more than one in five licensed drivers. Starting at 65 gives every driver a baseline while they are still sharp, so a decline later on shows up early instead of after a crash.

Source: NHTSA, citing FHWA 2022

They ask

"Does testing older drivers actually save lives?"

The facts

States that require older drivers to renew in person saw 25% lower fatal crash involvement among drivers 85 and older, in a study of 46 states over 26 years. After Florida began requiring vision tests at renewal, deaths among drivers 80 and older fell 17%.

Source: NHTSA, In-Person Renewal and Vision Test (Tefft 2014; McGwin et al. 2008)

They say

"Driver's licenses are a state issue. Washington can't do this."

The facts

States issue licenses, and they will keep doing so. Congress can still set a national standard through its spending power, by tying federal highway funds to states that adopt it. That is how the 21 drinking age became national in 1984, and the Supreme Court upheld it in South Dakota v. Dole (1987).

Source: South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987)

They ask

"Does any state already do this?"

The facts

Yes, but every state does it differently. Illinois, one of the strictest, requires a vision test from age 79, in-person renewal every two years from 81, and a yearly road test from 87. Most states ask far less. A driver's safety check should not depend on which side of a state line they live on.

Source: WTTW News, 2026

They say

"Taking away a senior's license takes away their independence."

The facts

We take that seriously. That is why the act targets drivers who are unfit, not drivers who are older. Testing protects the independence of the many seniors who drive well, and it protects the pedestrians, cyclists and other drivers who share the road with the few who no longer can.

Sample message to your lawmakers

Dear Representative,

I am writing to ask for your support of the Senior Driver Safety Act. From 2001 to 2021, the share of fatal crashes involving drivers 65 and older rose from 11% to 19%. The act would require regular driving and skill tests for drivers 65 and older and create a national point system, so that drivers who are no longer fit to drive are identified before a crash happens. Drivers who pass keep driving. This is about safety for everyone on the road, seniors included.

Thank you for your time.
[Your name, city and state]
